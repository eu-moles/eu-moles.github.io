2026-07-07Second readingTemporary derogation from certain provisions of the ePrivacy Directive to combat online child sexual abuse

Discussion transcript

Temporary derogation from certain provisions of the ePrivacy Directive to combat online child sexual abuse

Where available, remarks are machine-translated into English. Select the language badge to view the original.

President

The first vote is on the request by the EPP Group for an urgent decision on the temporary derogation from certain provisions of the ePrivacy Directive to combat online child sexual abuse (see minutes, item 7.1).

I give the floor first to Tomas Tobé to speak in favour of the urgent decision.

Tomas Tobé

Madam President, dear colleagues, today's vote is not about party politics. Today's vote is not about majority tactics or strategy. Today's vote is not about shared control. It is not even about procedures. It is much more important than that. Today's vote is about deciding if we want to protect our children, or if we want to protect sexual predators.

Madam President, in March, we collectively failed to protect children. But in April, this House, together, clearly urged the Commission to come up with measures to close the legal gap that we currently have. The Commission has acted, and 26 out of 27 Member States have agreed.

Colleagues, we have a unique chance right now to send a clear signal – we care. We care about our children, and we will protect them because behind every child that is exposed on sexual abuse materials online is a victim that is never heard, and an offender that is never brought to justice. Without the interim regulation, 99 % of child abuse material goes undetected. This is unacceptable. We cannot go to summer recess knowing that our children are not protected.

So I say to all you colleagues, please take responsibility today. Vote today in favour of the urgent resolution to protect children on Thursday. And then we continue our work together on the permanent regulation. Vote to protect children.

President

I give the floor now to Mary Khan to speak against.

Mary Khan

Madam President! Summer, sun, mass surveillance. This is how some EU elites imagine the start of the summer holidays. Shortly before the break, a monitoring structure should be decided quickly. And this despite the fact that Parliament has already rejected chat control. Now the same suggestion is simply being rehashed.

In this House of Democracy, people apparently vote until the result is correct. You all know full well that there is no majority for permanent chat control. That's why they're now trying, together with the council, to revive the whole thing through the back door, which is why, as is so often the case, they're always choosing the salami tactic.

Translated, this means: Private messages should be made technically searchable and images, videos and even texts should be checked automatically. The fact that people are trying to get the whole thing through with an urgent motion today shows the spirit of this project.

Ursula von der Leyen has been trying to get access to our private messages since 2021. She herself cannot disclose her SMS: Everyone knows what was going on on our continent in 2021 during the Corona measures period.

And of course – this argument doesn't work, I'm a mother myself – of course we want to protect all children. But real child protection does not start with placing every citizen under general suspicion. Real child protection begins where perpetrators really become dangerous: with harsh punishments instead of probation; there needs to be open registers of perpetrators; with better law enforcement and the dismantling of grooming gangs. Anyone who treats child molesters with kid gloves but wants to place millions of citizens under general suspicion is deliberately setting their priorities wrong.

And to every citizen: Every citizen can and should see today which MP voted today to make their private messages searchable in the future. Take a close look! Remember the names! Every MP who votes for this emergency motion is also voting for mass surveillance.

2026-07-07A coherent tax framework for the EU's financial sector

Discussion transcript

A coherent tax framework for the EU's financial sector

Where available, remarks are machine-translated into English. Select the language badge to view the original.

President

The next vote is on a coherent tax framework for the EU's financial sector (see minutes, item 7.16).

Matthias Ecke

Madam President, colleagues, we cannot carry on like this. This is not sustainable. Our financial sector taxation system needs reform. Europe needs more investment for our digital and green transitions to strengthen our security and to foster investment. We need a deeper integrated financial market, but our taxation system has not kept up with that challenge. The VAT exemption on financial services dates back to 1977. This is nearly 50 years and it was designed for an entirely different financial system. Today, it creates distortions and it undermines our single market.

There is no simple solution for that. This is why this report is proposing no specific recipe, but it calls on the Commission to thoroughly investigate the matter and to propose a coherent framework. And to be honest, colleagues, I expect ambition. I expect the Commission to also assess the role that financial transaction tax and windfall taxes can play in such a framework. We should also take a hard look on whether national tax regimes for the financial sector contribute to harmful tax competition in the European Union and sustain tax havens.

Colleagues, the shortcomings of the current system have led to more than 90 different national sectoral taxations – more than 90 – across the Union. So, this patchwork creates unnecessary complexity and bureaucracy. It distorts the single market and it ultimately weakens European competitiveness. After almost 50 years, it's time for a change. It's time to modernise. I want you to be part of that modernisation. I am asking for your support.

2026-07-07A new strategy for media literacy and digital learning

Discussion transcript

A new strategy for media literacy and digital learning

Where available, remarks are machine-translated into English. Select the language badge to view the original.

President

The next vote is on a new strategy for media literacy and digital learning (see minutes, item 7.17).

Marcos Ros Sempere

Señora presidenta, durante mucho tiempo vimos la expansión de internet y de las redes sociales con optimismo. Pensábamos que la información circularía libre y que la conversación pública sería más abierta. Sin embargo, el espacio digital que nos atraviesa por completo ha cambiado radicalmente y ese optimismo se ha nublado. La concentración de poder en pocas manos, la opacidad y un modelo de negocio basado en la adicción permanente ha transformado profundamente ese ecosistema. La desinformación circula con facilidad, los contenidos manipulados se multiplican y la inteligencia artificial ha acelerado y multiplicado este efecto.

Conocer cómo funciona este nuevo mundo debe ser hoy una prioridad democrática. Hace décadas, entendimos que no existía la ciudadanía plena sin saber leer y escribir. Hoy, nos ocurre algo parecido: en la era digital, nadie puede ejercer plenamente sus derechos si no distingue la realidad de la desinformación, si no reconoce una manipulación generada por inteligencia artificial o si no entiende cómo funcionan los algoritmos. Y si nuestra ciudadanía no tiene esas herramientas, nuestras democracias no están preparadas para esta nueva era digital.

Señorías, ningún Estado miembro puede afrontar este desafío por sí solo. Por eso, hoy votamos implementar una nueva estrategia de alfabetización mediática y de aprendizaje digital para toda Europa. Una estrategia que reconozca competencias como básicas y esenciales para toda la ciudadanía, a cualquier edad y a lo largo de toda la vida, especialmente para niños, niñas y adolescentes. Servirá para su protección frente al acoso, los contenidos inapropiados y el diseño adictivo, también para nuestros mayores y el conjunto de la ciudadanía, porque nadie puede ser libre en un entorno digital que no entiende.

La alfabetización mediática y digital es una necesidad democrática y hoy enviamos un mensaje claro. Gracias por su voto.

2026-07-06Agenda requestRecent violent and fatal attacks in Italy and Denmark and their implications for public security in Europe

Discussion transcript

Recent violent and fatal attacks in Italy and Denmark and their implications for public security in Europe

Where available, remarks are machine-translated into English. Select the language badge to view the original.

President

The next item is the debate on Parliament's statements on recent violent and fatal attacks in Italy and Denmark and their implications for public security in Europe (2026/2814(RSP) ).

As you know, it's one round for political groups.

Tomas Tobé

Mr Speaker! Colleagues! Today I want to talk about something terrible that shook Sweden. A Swedish citizen and police officer, Christian Zedig, has been murdered in Copenhagen in a brutal and senseless act of violence. A normal holiday evening and football celebration turned into a tragedy, and a family has now been left in great sadness and loss. A wife who has lost her husband and two children who have lost their father. Our thoughts go out to them, to other relatives, colleagues and friends.

When violence breaks out, trust in our communities is damaged. In cities, squares and events around Europe, we should be able to move freely without fear. Respect for law and order is not only a national project but common European values.

People who do not have the right to be in Europe should not be able to threaten our security. Ultimately, it is about defending our European values ​​and our way of life. Rest in peace.

Susanna Ceccardi

Mr President, ladies and gentlemen, how many more victims will it take before Europe finds the courage to look reality in the eye? How many more?

In Milan, a 55 year-old man was stabbed over 20 times while having breakfast with his father. The attacker, a young man born in Italy to Gambian parents, declared after his arrest: "I had fun. As soon as I get out, I'll do it again."

In Sweden, a 32 year-old policeman was murdered by a man of African descent with a criminal record while watching a World Cup match.

They are the face of a Europe that for too long has preferred to look the other way.

In too many cases, the perpetrators of this violence are young people born or raised in Europe, children of immigration. The truth is that integration is not proclaimed, it is built and when it fails, innocent citizens pay the price.

For years they told us that it was enough to welcome, that everything would resolve itself, that reporting these problems was intolerance. Today the facts deny this ideology.

But now it is no longer enough to just talk about stopping illegal immigration, the time has come to talk about remigration: the European Union must organize a true European remigration policy, also through Frontex.

For years we have seen ships busy bringing migrants to Europe. Now we need to reverse course. European means and resources must also be used to bring back to their countries of origin those who do not have the right to remain on our territory. The expulsions really need to be carried out. Remigration must become a priority for European politics.

For too long Europe has been concerned with not offending anyone rather than protecting its citizens, but the first freedom is to live without fear, the first right is security and the first duty of a State is to protect its people.

It's time to protect our borders, our cities, our citizens: it's time to reverse course.

Giuseppe Lupo

Mr President, ladies and gentlemen, the issue of public security in Europe certainly deserves the utmost attention and is a major emergency in some areas of my country - Italy - in particular with regard to the fight against organized crime and mafias, which are still strong and present in the area.

My region is Sicily, the land of Giovanni Falcone and Paolo Borsellino, of many women and men who gave their lives to fight the mafia and affirm legality.

In particular, in recent months, my city - Palermo - has recorded numerous episodes of violence linked to the mafia phenomenon: several commercial businesses have suffered violent acts of intimidation, some have been set on fire and organized crime has repeatedly fired assault rifles, better known as Kalashnikovs, to intimidate honest entrepreneurs and induce them to submit to the mafia's logic.

Some entrepreneurs have recently been assigned the escort for reasons of personal safety and security, as recently happened to local administrators, most recently a councilor of my municipality, Palermo.

Faced with all this, the Meloni government's response so far has been, in my opinion, completely unsatisfactory. Minister Piantedosi has announced the strengthening of a video camera system, but we are waiting for concrete measures to strengthen the staff of the police and the judiciary, for an effective fight against mafia crime, which extends its criminal affairs throughout Europe.

Also for this reason - I conclude - I hope that the directive concerning the recovery and confiscation of assets will soon be implemented by all member states, in order to produce effective results, just as the La Torre law on the confiscation of assets confiscated from the mafia has produced them in Italy.

Charlie Weimers

Mr Speaker! It could have been me. A nice summer evening in Copenhagen: beer, friends, WC on the big screen - hard to imagine anything better. That was how the evening was meant for Christian Zedig. He was 32 years old, the father of two small children and a police officer. He was off that day. He was just trying to calm down a fight, and that's when the immigrant gang turned on him. He was knocked down and stomped on the head repeatedly. He was killed because he was the one who took responsibility.

Mr Speaker! In the same week, an innocent man in Milan was stabbed 20 times by a Gambian immigrant. Gerardo was having breakfast with his father in a cafe and it could have been any of us in here. Two attacks in two countries, but the same issue that we can no longer turn a blind eye to. When things like this happen again and again, then we must dare to speak about it.

Yet the Social Democrats, the Left and the Greens voted against allowing the European Parliament to even hold a debate on these attacks and what they mean for security in Europe. You red-greens don't even want to discuss it, and you were the ones who couldn't even get behind the return regulation - a law that will make it easier to send foreign violent criminals who commit serious crimes here in Europe.

You refuse to talk about the problems. You refuse to do anything about it. This is not a political game, this is real. Behind every attack is a family that will never be the same again. There are children who lose their father. We owe Christian, Gerardo and all the victims more than empty words. We owe them action.

Abir Al-Sahlani

Mr Speaker! The World Cup should be a public celebration, a place for joy, community and hope. Instead, a Swedish football supporter traveled to experience football and never came home. To Christian's family and friends, I want to express my deepest sorrow and my warmest sympathy. May you find comfort and light in this bottomless sorrow.

When a terrible crime like this takes place, anger, sadness and fear are aroused. It is only human, but it is precisely in such cases and moments that the rule of law is tested. We as politicians are not the judges. We are not the prosecutors, and we are not the ones to determine guilt or punishment. We are legislators. Our responsibility is to ensure that people can live in safety, to give the police and justice system the tools they need to prevent, investigate and prosecute those who commit the crimes, and to defend the rule of law even when emotions are at their strongest.

That is why it is so remarkable that Charlie Weimers of the Sweden Democrats once again chooses to use the tragic death of an individual to score political points. When an ongoing criminal case becomes a political bludgeon, the focus shifts from the victim and becomes a rhetorical prop. Christian deserves better. His family deserves better and this House deserves better.

This chamber is capable of more than this level. Let the judiciary do its work. Let's honor Christian with respect and not use his memory as a political prop. Let us be the legislators our citizens expect us to be: principled, accountable and worthy of the trust they have placed in us.

Alexander Sell

Mr. President! 80 stabbings a day, over 1,000 deaths a year – that is the situation in Germany. But today we're talking about Italy and Denmark. We don't even talk about Germany anymore because we've gotten used to the violence. Solingen, Mannheim, Aschaffenburg, police officers, train attendants, children - all over Germany, innocent people are becoming victims of senseless violence. But not only knives are used, but also firearms more and more often; There are shootings in Berlin every day. In Stade in Lower Saxony, six people were shot a week ago in a mother and child home - murdered in cold blood because they wanted to help a child.

Who are the perpetrators? Here too, the police crime statistics are clear: Syrians and Afghans are ten times more likely to be suspects in serious violent crimes than Germans. Criminal Syrian clans are spreading massive violence in Dortmund, Bremen and Berlin.

I ask you: How can it be that people who supposedly fled a war become criminals here? How can it be that people who get everything from us – housing, citizens’ benefit, health insurance; Over half of the Syrians in Germany live on citizens' money - how can it be that these people murder, rob and rape here?

Because they have a different culture. Because they consider our generosity to be weakness, and because they put their family honor above our justice and our law.

There is therefore only one answer to the escalating violence: remigration. We have to return these people to their homeland. Anyone who is attacked must defend themselves.

Lukas Sieper

Mr. President! I was almost certain that it would happen, Mr. Sell; I really would have bet a month's salary on it.

Mr President, I would like to use legal comparison to carry out the legal analysis of why this is a breach of Rules 10(3) and 10(4) of the Rules of Procedure. My colleague Weimers said things that I may not be able to fully follow, but that are part of a respectful debate. Mr Sell said that these people rob, murder and rape because they have a different culture. That was the statement.

That is the difference between a tough political debate and racist abuse, and that is what we cannot tolerate in this House. That's why I ask you and the Presidium - can you please keep your mouth shut when I'm talking, Mr. Jungbluth? That's why I ask you to confront this, to examine it, because despite all the justified concern, despite all the justified anger, there can be no room for racism in this House because people are being murdered.

President

Thank you very much, Mr Sieper.

(in response to comments from Tomasz Froelich)

Please, please. We are not in a debate. Please. We are respecting your turns. We are respecting the turns of the debate. If you are not able to listen to people that think differently than you, leave this plenary, please. He has the right to raise this point.

My message to Mr Sieper is, as you must know, we always, in all the cases, check carefully what is being said here in this plenary.

The debate is closed.
